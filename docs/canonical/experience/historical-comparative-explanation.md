# Historical & Comparative Explanation

**Canonical key:** `experience.historical-comparative-explanation`

**Kind:** EXPERIENCE CONTRACT

**Authority:** CANDIDATE / NOT CURRENT AUTHORITY

**Migration record:** `stable_family.EXPL`

**Owns current question:** How are historical and comparative Explanations bound, compared and disclosed without rewriting earlier knowledge or conflating retained communication with reconstruction?

**Stable IDs:** EXPL-141–EXPL-160

## Current semantics

### EXPL-141 — Comparative Request & View Binding
Bind each comparison side independently to requested proposition, subject/scope, event/effective window and knowledge perspective rather than assuming one side's coordinates apply to the other.

### EXPL-142 — Event/Effective Time, Knowledge Cut & Communication Time
Keep source event/effective time, framework knowledge cut and communication time independent; later communication does not imply later knowledge existed earlier.

### EXPL-143 — Historical Source State
Historical source state is the source-owned fact/action/result effective for the historical proposition and remains distinct from whether it was known or communicated at the time.

### EXPL-144 — As-Known-at-Cut Explanation
An as-known Explanation uses only evidence eligible by cutoff K and preserves unknown/not-evidenced states that were legitimate at that cut.

### EXPL-145 — Retained Actual Communication
When retained, actual historical communication records what was really communicated to a particular audience/context; it is not interchangeable with reconstructed answerability.

### EXPL-146 — Current Retrospective Explanation
Current retrospective Explanation may use later accepted evidence/corrections about the historical proposition while explicitly preserving that such evidence was learned later.

### EXPL-147 — Current Authorization Over Historical Views
Current requester authorization independently governs present disclosure of historical source/as-known/retained/retrospective views and does not rewrite historical actor authorization.

### EXPL-148 — Comparable Statement Identity
Treat statements as same-proposition comparable only when material subject, predicate/conclusion, scope and event target are equivalent; changed proposition identity is not one statement's status transition.

### EXPL-149 — Typed Explanation Diff
Classify differences across source/status, evidence/basis, knowledge cut, derivation, scope, materiality, authorization/detail, presentation and retained-versus-reconstructed dimensions.

### EXPL-150 — Truth/Status vs Knowledge/Basis Change
Distinguish source proposition/status change from newly available basis or changed knowledge; learning more about unchanged truth is not the same as truth changing.

### EXPL-151 — Authorization, Detail & Presentation Change
More or less visible/detail-rich wording can reflect authorization or presentation change without implying stronger/weaker truth.

### EXPL-152 — Addition, Removal, Materiality & Historical Negative Discipline
Statement addition/removal or changing materiality does not by itself mean truth changed; historical unknown/not-evidenced/not-recorded/unavailable/restricted and evidence-backed negatives remain distinct.

### EXPL-153 — Comparative Health, Change & Execution Semantics
Historical health/change/execution comparisons preserve exact HLTH/OPS proposition identities and boundaries rather than flattening different periods into one generic before/after status.

### EXPL-154 — Comparative Investigation, Causality & Impact Semantics
Compare Investigation/localization, Causal Claim and Impact layers independently; later confirmation or exposure evidence never backfills into earlier as-known Explanation.

### EXPL-155 — Comparative Control & Governance Semantics
Compare Safeguard/Gate/authorization/authority/governance state using their exact action, decision, enforcement and knowledge histories without inventing retrospective control effectiveness or compliance.

### EXPL-156 — Correction & Supersession Across Historical Views
Correction/supersession changes the current preferred interpretation while preserving earlier source state, knowledge-cut conclusions and retained communication as historical records.

### EXPL-157 — Multi-Cut Progression
Support proposition-specific progression across multiple knowledge cuts without converting increasing evidence/detail into a universal maturity, confidence or completeness score.

### EXPL-158 — Why Did the Explanation Change?
Explain answer differences through typed descriptive deltas such as new evidence, corrected source state, changed scope/materiality or authorization; domain causal attribution still requires Causal Claim semantics.

### EXPL-159 — Consolidated Explanation Contract
A valid Explanation is a bounded, source-faithful, basis-traceable, epistemically calibrated and currently authorized communication projection whose evolution and historical views remain non-rewriting.

### EXPL-160 — Phase 008 Exit & Integration Handoff
Provide CKR-H/Phase 009 integration work with exact functional requirements for source availability, identity/join keys, timestamps, authority, coverage, retention, latency, authorization/disclosure and inspectability without weakening Explanation semantics for integration convenience.

## Invariants / boundaries

- historical source state ≠ as-known-at-cut Explanation ≠ retained actual communication ≠ current retrospective Explanation;
- event/effective time ≠ knowledge cut ≠ communication time;
- retained communication ≠ reconstructed what-was-explainable;
- later evidence never becomes earlier knowledge;
- current authorization governs present disclosure, not historical truth;
- materially changed proposition ≠ one proposition's status update;
- more detail/visibility ≠ more truth;
- statement removal ≠ false/retracted by default;
- authentic prior answer can be unsuitable now without becoming fictitious;
- `why did the Explanation change` ≠ domain causality by default;
- no EXPL-161 or universal replay/maturity/confidence score.

## Architecture boundary

This contract does not select presentation, LLM, retrieval, persistence, integration, snapshot, diff-rendering or technical architecture.
